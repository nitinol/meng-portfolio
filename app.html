01 / Set the conditions

Prepare

Turn product intent into a release-ready scope.

  • Define the customer outcome, requirements, release scope and readiness criteria.
  • Distinguish a new app launch from an update; agree the build version, dependencies and release window.
  • Surface risks early and define go/no-go owners, escalation paths and hotfix readiness.

Carry forward An agreed release brief.

02 / Align the people

Coordinate

Make dependencies visible before they become delays.

  • Align Product, Development and QA on scope, testing, acceptance and the decision to ship.
  • Connect UX/UI assets, Marketing positioning and Campaign/CRM timing to the release plan.
  • Agree analytics coverage, store responsibilities and post-release monitoring ownership.

Carry forward A shared plan with named owners.

03 / Shape the storefront

Configure

Translate one release into three store-ready packages.

  • Prepare localized metadata, screenshots, descriptions and release notes that match the actual product.
  • Check build version and signing, supported devices, availability and store-specific requirements.
  • Coordinate privacy disclosures, permissions, age ratings and compliance information with accountable teams.

Carry forward Consistent listings, ecosystem-specific configuration.

04 / Earn the go decision

Validate

Make readiness an evidence-based decision.

  • Coordinate functional, regression and device testing with QA and Development.
  • Check critical journeys, analytics events, crash reporting and performance against agreed acceptance criteria.
  • Review screenshots, metadata, privacy and compliance; resolve blockers before submission.

Carry forward A documented go/no-go decision.

05 / Navigate the review

Submit

Own the review loop, not just the submit button.

  • Coordinate submission through each store’s console with the correct build, listing and review information.
  • Track review feedback, clarify questions and route rejections to the right owner for correction and resubmission.
  • Keep stakeholders informed: approval and production availability are different milestones.

Carry forward Reviewed packages and a publication plan.

06 / Move into production

Release

Control exposure and confirm the customer experience.

  • Coordinate publication, phased rollout where supported, market availability and production checks.
  • Align campaign activation and support readiness with actual storefront availability, not an assumed review date.
  • If signals deteriorate, halt further rollout where supported and coordinate remediation; installed binaries are not rolled back by the store.

Carry forward ✓ Conceptual release complete → monitoring.

07 / Stay with the release

Monitor

Publication is a handoff to observation, not the finish line.

  • Review crashes, performance and critical production journeys with Development and Release Monitoring.
  • Read adoption, analytics, ratings and customer feedback together; distinguish release issues from broader trends.
  • Escalate incidents with named owners and decide whether to pause exposure or prepare a hotfix.

Carry forward Prioritized signals, not just a published build.

08 / Close the loop

Improve

Bring release learning back into product decisions.

  • Prioritize fixes and improvements from customer feedback, adoption and reliability signals.
  • Coordinate a tested hotfix build and a new store submission when needed; confirm availability after review.
  • Review the operation with the teams and improve readiness checks, handoffs and the next release plan.

Carry forward A better starting point. Return to Prepare ↗
